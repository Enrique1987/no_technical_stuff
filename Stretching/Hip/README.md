# Hip

A gradual hip-mobility path, from very gentle movements to more demanding exercises. Start with the current level only and progress when it feels controlled and comfortable.

## Level 01 — Seated Heel Slide

**Goal:** Gently introduce hip movement without lifting the leg or asking for deep hip bend.

**Why this is the starting point:** The foot stays on the floor and can move only a few centimetres. It is a simpler starting point than seated marching or deep knee-to-chest positions.

### Setup

- Use a solid, stable chair without wheels.
- Sit near the front of the chair with both feet flat on the floor, about hip-width apart.
- If the floor is not slippery, place a small towel or paper plate under one foot so it can glide easily.
- Hold the sides of the chair if that helps you feel stable.

### Movement

1. Slowly slide one heel forward along the floor as far as is comfortable. Do not force the range.
2. Pause for one calm breath.
3. Slide the foot back to the starting position.
4. Repeat with the other foot.

### Dose

- Start with **5 slow repetitions per side**, once a day.
- Keep the movement small. Quality and comfort matter more than distance.
- When this feels easy and does not make symptoms worse later that day or the next day, build gradually to 2 sets of 5 per side.

### Stop and get advice

Stop if you feel sharp pain, increasing pain, numbness, tingling, or loss of control. Seek urgent medical advice after a fall, if you cannot bear weight, or if the hip is hot/swollen or pain begins suddenly and severely. If hip pain disrupts sleep or normal activities, or fails to improve, consult a clinician or physiotherapist before progressing.

### Notes

- This is a mobility drill, not a deep stretch.
- Breathe normally and keep the motion slow and controlled.
- The next level can be added only when this level is comfortable.
