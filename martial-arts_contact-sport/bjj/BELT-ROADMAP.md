# BJJ belt checklist for adults

A personal study plan from **white to black belt**, covering gi and no-gi where relevant. This is not an official curriculum: each instructor decides promotions. The IBJJF regulates graduation and competition but does not publish a universal list of techniques required at each belt. The checklist is a suggested progression and **is cumulative**: blue belts should continue practising the white-belt material.

## How to complete a checkbox

- Mark a technique complete when you can **explain its purpose, perform it with control on both sides and recognize its defense**. For application tasks, test it in positional sparring with progressive resistance. You do not need to succeed every time.
- For advanced guards, passes and submissions, a checkbox that says **choose** requires one functional system from that family, rather than every variation. Record your selected variation in your [class notes](classes/).
- **Cardio and conditioning** items describe observable habits and abilities. They are not medical tests or promotion thresholds. Adapt training load, rest and exercises to your condition and your coach's guidance.
- Practise leg, neck and wrist locks under supervision. Before using one in competition, check the current rules for the organization, belt, age group and division. **Studying a technique does not mean it is legal in your category.**

## White belt: foundations for blue belt

### Safety, rules and positional awareness

- [ ] I know when to tap and release immediately when my partner taps or the coach stops the action.
- [ ] I distinguish chokes, joint locks, pressure and control, and never apply a submission explosively.
- [ ] I recognize closed guard, open guard, half guard, side control, north-south, mount, back control, turtle and front headlock; I know who is in danger and where the exit lies.
- [ ] I know the basic positional hierarchy and the elementary scoring for takedowns, sweeps, passes, mount and back control under my academy's ruleset.
- [ ] I can train with partners of different sizes and intensities without striking, twisting fingers or landing on the neck or head.

### Movement, defense and escapes

- [ ] I perform a bridge, shrimp, hip switch, technical stand-up, sprawl and safe breakfall with control.
- [ ] I use frames, tight elbows, neck protection and my knees to create space; I do not push with straight arms while trapped.
- [ ] From side control, I recover half guard or full guard using frames, hip movement and a knee shield; I recognize the transition to north-south.
- [ ] From mount, I practise the bridge-and-roll escape and elbow-knee escape without giving up my back without a plan.
- [ ] From back control, I defend my neck first, control the choking hand and escape toward a safe side.
- [ ] From turtle, I protect my neck and back, recover guard or stand without exposing an easy choke.
- [ ] I recognize and defend an armbar, kimura, americana, triangle, guillotine and rear-naked choke early, tapping before forcing a joint.

### Guard, sweeps and stand-ups

- [ ] Closed guard: I break posture, manage distance and recover the closed position when my opponent tries to open it.
- [ ] Basic open guard: I place useful feet or hooks, manage distance and return my legs in front after a pass attempt.
- [ ] Half guard: I distinguish the underhook and crossface, avoid staying flat and can recover full guard or come up to one side.
- [ ] Butterfly guard: I establish hooks and an angled posture without being flattened.
- [ ] I perform the scissor sweep and hip-bump sweep from closed guard.
- [ ] I perform a pendulum or flower sweep from closed guard and one half-guard sweep using an underhook or knee lever.
- [ ] I perform a basic butterfly sweep and can use a technical stand-up if my opponent disengages.

### Passing, control, standing and attacks

- [ ] I open closed guard while maintaining posture and protecting my arms and neck.
- [ ] I attempt an outside toreando pass and an inside knee-cut pass, understanding why I must control the legs and hips.
- [ ] I pass half guard with pressure without trapping my arm or losing my base.
- [ ] I stabilize side control, mount and back control before submitting, following the hips when my opponent escapes.
- [ ] Standing: I use posture, distance and grips, break grips and enter a safe single leg or an equivalent takedown taught in class.
- [ ] I defend a leg entry with a sprawl and know how to fall without posting on my head or a locked arm.
- [ ] I finish an armbar, kimura and americana with control from an appropriate position.
- [ ] I finish a triangle, guillotine and rear-naked choke with control and know the initial defense to each.
- [ ] If I train gi, I practise the cross-collar choke and recognize the bow-and-arrow choke from the back.
- [ ] I recognize a straight ankle lock and its positional defense, applying it only when permitted and supervised.

### Cardio, effort management and study

- [ ] I finish the usual class rounds at an appropriate intensity, stopping when necessary and avoiding a sprint in every exchange.
- [ ] I notice when anxiety makes me grip or squeeze unnecessarily, release excess tension and return to controlled breathing once the position stabilizes.
- [ ] I distinguish fatigue from technical danger: when posture or control fails, I slow down, defend and request a pause if needed.
- [ ] I maintain sustainable attendance and recovery habits and record one specific position or mistake after training.

**Personal test:** start underneath side control, mount and back control, then escape or improve position against moderate resistance. From guard, sweep or stand. From the top, pass, stabilize and attack without losing control.

## Blue belt: foundations for purple belt

### Defense and retention

- [ ] I chain two escapes when the first fails: side control → half guard → guard, mount → half guard or back control → side control.
- [ ] I prevent a crossface and head control before being flattened in half guard.
- [ ] I retain guard against toreando, knee-cut and pressure passes, recovering hooks or half guard after losing the first barrier.
- [ ] I defend triangle → armbar → omoplata and kimura → back-control chains, recognizing the threat before it closes.
- [ ] I distinguish leg-control positions such as ashi garami and single-leg X, prioritizing knee-line clearance and foot protection according to technique and rules.

### Guards and sweeps

- [ ] I have a functional closed-guard sequence: break posture → attack or sweep → react to the defense.
- [ ] I have a half-guard sequence: underhook → sweep or back take, and can close space again after a crossface.
- [ ] I have a butterfly sequence: elevation or arm drag → sweep, back take or transition to X-guard.
- [ ] I choose one long-range open guard and develop its entry, retention, sweep and exit: collar-sleeve or spider for gi, or De La Riva / reverse De La Riva as appropriate.
- [ ] I understand X-guard and single-leg X mechanics and perform one sweep or stand-up from either position.
- [ ] I transition between guards when posture or grips change instead of remaining in a failed guard.

### Passing, control and standing

- [ ] I connect an outside pass such as toreando or leg drag with an inside knee cut when my opponent recovers.
- [ ] I choose one pressure pass, such as body lock, over-under or double under, and control the hips until stabilization.
- [ ] I stop inversion or guard recovery during a pass and switch sides without losing useful pressure.
- [ ] From side control, I mount or take the back when my opponent turns; from mount, I maintain base during a bridge.
- [ ] From the back, I maintain a seatbelt and hooks or a body triangle when appropriate and safe.
- [ ] Standing, I add a double leg, arm drag to the back and snap-down to front headlock, connecting a failed attempt to another action.
- [ ] I defend single-leg and double-leg attacks with posture, hips and grips and recover a safe position after falling.
- [ ] If I train gi, I use a basic grip → off-balance → sweep or throw sequence taught by my instructor.

### Attacks and chains

- [ ] I chain triangle ↔ armbar ↔ omoplata without opening large gaps.
- [ ] I use the kimura both as a submission and as control for a sweep or back take.
- [ ] I finish an arm triangle and one choke from north-south or front headlock with control.
- [ ] If I train gi, I finish a bow-and-arrow choke and know a loop choke or equivalent lapel choke.
- [ ] I perform a straight ankle lock without jerking and know its defenses, checking legality before competition use.
- [ ] I have at least one submission entry from guard, mount and back control, each with a second option when defended.

### Cardio and learning

- [ ] I distribute effort across the round, accelerating for a specific action and stabilizing, breathing and releasing tension at other times.
- [ ] I retain basic technique under moderate fatigue: posture, frames, neck protection and useful grips.
- [ ] I distinguish a cardio limitation from a technical failure, recording the position where effort spikes and drilling it specifically.
- [ ] I plan at least one positional-training block for a specific weakness and check whether it improves in later rounds.

**Personal test:** show a chain of two or three actions from guard and another while passing; recover guard against two types of pass; complete a purposeful takedown or guard pull and retain options after the first defense.

## Purple belt: foundations for brown belt

### Defense, guards and passing

- [ ] I defend chained leg-drag, body-lock, stack and knee-cut passes with early prevention and a recovery route.
- [ ] I escape mount, back control and side control against opponents who anticipate my first escape; I do not depend on a single reaction.
- [ ] I recognize entries and defenses for 50/50, deep half guard, lasso, advanced spider, lapel guards and K-guard, choosing those that support my game.
- [ ] My main guard works at long and short range: entry, retention, sweep, back take and exit when my opponent stands or applies pressure.
- [ ] I have one response to standing opponents and another to kneeling opponents from my primary guards.
- [ ] I combine outside, inside and pressure passing, using the opponent's defense to switch passes instead of always resetting.
- [ ] I connect pass → side control, mount or back → attack and keep position if the submission fails.

### Standing, control and attacks

- [ ] I chain takedown → pass or takedown → back control and have a response when my preferred entry is defended.
- [ ] I attack and defend from front headlock: snap-down, back take, guillotine or safe exit.
- [ ] I move my opponent between side control, north-south, mount and back control according to their escapes instead of chasing one submission.
- [ ] I finish and defend D'Arce and anaconda chokes from appropriate positions.
- [ ] I chain an armbar from mount or back control with a kimura or back transition.
- [ ] If I train gi, I develop two lapel chokes from different positions and their defenses.
- [ ] I understand outside ashi, inside ashi and 50/50 controls and early defenses against leg attacks.
- [ ] I recognize the kneebar, toe hold, calf slicer, heel hook and reaping, practising them **only** when my instructor and the ruleset permit them.

### Cardio, strategy and study

- [ ] I manage effort through several rounds, reserving explosiveness for entries, escapes and transitions and returning to a sustainable pace.
- [ ] I identify grips, positions and habits that tire me without creating advantage and replace them with structure, angle or transition.
- [ ] I have a plan A and plan B for guard, passing and standing and can describe which opponent response triggers each change.
- [ ] I analyze specific rounds: starting position, decision, opponent reaction, error and adjustment for the next session.

**Personal test:** impose a primary system against different styles, change plans when it is denied and defend an area outside your specialty.

## Brown belt: foundations for black belt

### Coverage and technical depth

- [ ] I have an initial solution and second option for standing, top and bottom guard, top and bottom half guard, side control, mount, back, turtle and front headlock.
- [ ] I escape and retain position against advanced controls without waiting for a submission to close.
- [ ] I pass guards I do not normally play and can explain which grip, hook or angle must be neutralized first.
- [ ] I maintain a connected guard network and abandon a variation once my opponent has neutralized it.
- [ ] I change pace, side and technique family according to the opponent's response rather than repeating a failed entry.
- [ ] I manage the end of a round and scoring scenarios without sacrificing safety or giving away positions through haste.
- [ ] I defend leg and back attacks with knowledge of their entries, controls and exits and review the relevant rules.

### Attacking, standing and teaching

- [ ] I have at least two reliable takedown or ground-entry chains, two guard chains, two passing chains and two submission chains that include responses to defense.
- [ ] I gain position before attacking the neck, arm or leg and release a failed attack without losing the entire advantage.
- [ ] I can teach a beginner the entry, control, finish, defense and common error for a fundamental technique.
- [ ] I adapt technique and intensity to my partner and recognize when a variation is unsuitable because of size, mobility or injury.
- [ ] I know the rules, scoring, advantages and fouls in the formats where I train or compete and verify changes before each event.

### Cardio and judgment

- [ ] I make technical decisions during demanding rounds without relying only on maximum strength and know when to pause, control and attack again.
- [ ] I adjust training volume and intensity to recover for important sessions and distinguish accumulated fatigue from one poor day.
- [ ] I can explain why my game consumes energy and which changes in position or grip make it more efficient.

**Personal test:** solve opponents with different profiles, retain options after the first attack fails and safely teach the fundamentals you use.

## Black belt: continuous competence checklist

- [ ] I keep fundamental escapes, guard, passing, control, takedowns and submissions functional as my primary game changes.
- [ ] I identify my blind spots and design positional practice to correct them using evidence from live rounds.
- [ ] I adapt my system to gi or no-gi, rules, age, physical condition and opponents without losing its core principles.
- [ ] I teach safe progressions: objective, entry, control, finish, defense and variations for different levels.
- [ ] I review rules and safety standards before teaching or competing with techniques whose legality varies.
- [ ] I maintain a sustainable balance of training, recovery and physical preparation and reassess my goals and abilities over time.

A black belt does not mean that every technique has been completed. Practice and review continue.

## Tracking each technique

Copy this block into a [class note](classes/) for each checkbox you are working on:

```text
Technique / problem:
Belt and section:
Entry and control:
Opponent's most common defense:
My second option:
Positional sparring test (date, position and resistance):
What failed / next adjustment:
Validated with instructor (date):
```

Work on a few checkboxes at a time and review their real application. The [video library](videos/) connects clips to checklist sections after representative frames have been reviewed. A video is useful for **observation or preparing a drill**; watching it does not complete a checkbox. Confirm details with your instructor and test them with progressive resistance.
