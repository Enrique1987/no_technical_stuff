# Muay Thai Class 01 — Progressive Partner Drills and Technical Sparring

**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**Format:** Partner drilling → progressive combinations → constrained sparring  
**Intensity:** Technical / controlled

This note preserves the class structure so it can be reused later for personal practice or teaching.

---

## 1. Warm-up

### Jump rope

- **10 minutes**

### Shadowboxing

- Free shadowboxing after the rope warm-up.
- Focus on stance, movement, balance, and clean technique before partner work.

---

## 2. Progressive partner drills

The drills were built progressively. One partner worked first, then the other partner repeated the same task.

The receiving partner could give a small, controlled response so the drill stayed realistic, but the purpose was **learning rather than winning the exchange**.

> The exact round length was not confirmed. It may have been around two minutes per partner, but this should not be treated as fixed.

### Drill 1 — Jab / Double Jab / Front Kick

Work through simple long-range attacks with a partner:

```text
Jab
Double Jab
Front Kick / Teep
```

The emphasis is on:

- distance,
- balance,
- clean entries,
- returning to stance,
- reading a light partner response.

---

## 3. Combination 1 — Jab → Low Kick → Jab → Cross

The first combination added punches and a low kick:

```text
Jab
→ Low Kick
→ Jab
→ Cross
```

Partners alternated roles and repeated the sequence.

### Coaching focus

- Do not pause completely after the low kick.
- Recover stance quickly enough to re-enter with the jab–cross.
- Keep the drill technical rather than power-focused.

---

## 4. Combination 2 — Left Hook → Right Cross → Switch Left Middle Kick

The next combination added a change of angle and a switch kick:

```text
Left Hook
→ Right Cross
→ Switch Step
→ Left Middle Kick
```

For an orthodox stance:

- **Left Hook** = lead hook.
- **Right Cross** = rear straight.
- **Switch Left Middle Kick** = switch stance, then kick with the left leg to the body.

### Coaching focus

- Keep the hook compact.
- Let the right cross flow naturally into the switch.
- Do not over-jump the switch step.
- Finish balanced after the middle kick.

---

## 5. Pressure / “Zombie” drill

One partner advanced forward in a straight line, like a **zombie**, applying simple continuous pressure.

The defending partner could use the combinations already trained, but now had to react to forward movement.

A key defensive action was:

```text
Opponent advances straight
→ Move off the line / angle out
→ Right Cross
```

The objective was not simply to retreat backward.

The defender had to:

1. recognize the forward pressure,
2. move laterally off the center line,
3. create a safer angle,
4. answer with the right hand.

### Main lesson

> Do not stay directly in front of a forward-moving opponent.

Create an angle, then counter.

---

## 6. Sparring progression — approximately 30%

The class finished with three different technical sparring rounds.

### Round 1 — Boxing only

Allowed:

- punches,
- footwork,
- defensive movement.

No kicks, knees, or other Muay Thai weapons.

### Round 2 — Kickboxing

Add kicks to the boxing work.

Focus on combining:

- punches,
- kicks,
- movement,
- distance management.

### Round 3 — Muay Thai

Open the rules further to Muay Thai techniques.

The overall intensity stayed around **30%**, with the priority on technical decision-making rather than power.

---

# Main skills trained

- Basic forward striking with a partner.
- Jab and double-jab entries.
- Front kick / teep distance management.
- Punch-to-low-kick transitions.
- Re-entering with punches after a kick.
- Lead hook → rear straight mechanics.
- Switch-step left middle kick.
- Moving laterally instead of retreating only in a straight line.
- Countering pressure after creating an angle.
- Gradual progression from constrained sparring to full Muay Thai rules.
- Maintaining low intensity while still making real decisions.
